Unfiled returns

Unfiled tax returns: get current with the IRS.

Missing tax returns can lead to growing penalties and IRS-prepared returns that leave out your deductions. Filing is usually the first step toward any relief.

Why it matters

Why you should file even if you can't pay

Bigger penalties for not filing

The failure-to-file penalty is generally 5% of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25%, ten times the monthly late-payment penalty.

Substitute for return

If you don't file, the IRS may prepare a return for you using only the income it knows about, without the deductions and credits you may be entitled to.

Lost refunds

If you were owed a refund, you generally have only three years from the original due date to claim it.

Access to relief

Payment plans, Offers in Compromise, and penalty relief generally require that all required returns be filed first.

How far back you may need to file

The IRS generally asks for the last six years of returns to consider you in compliance, though your situation may call for more or fewer. We help you figure out exactly what's required.

If the IRS already filed for you

You can usually replace an IRS-prepared substitute return by filing your own accurate return, which can lower the balance the IRS says you owe.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I don't file my tax returns?+

Penalties and interest keep growing, the IRS may file a return for you without your deductions, and you may lose refunds. Eventually it can lead to collection action such as levies.

How many years of back taxes do I need to file?+

The IRS generally looks for the last six years of returns to bring you into compliance, but it depends on your situation.

Can I file back taxes if I can't pay what I owe?+

Yes, and you should. Filing stops the failure-to-file penalty from growing, and it opens the door to payment plans and other relief options.
