Penalty abatement (penalty relief)

IRS penalty abatement: reduce or remove IRS penalties.

Penalties and the interest charged on them can make a tax bill grow fast. If you qualify, the IRS may remove some or all of them.

Common penalties

IRS penalties that may be reduced

These are the penalties people ask us about most.

Failure to file

Charged when a return is filed late. It's generally 5% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month the return is late, up to 25%.

Failure to pay

Charged when tax isn't paid by the due date. It's generally 0.5% of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25%.

Failure to deposit

Charged to businesses when payroll taxes aren't deposited on time or in the right way.

Accuracy-related

Charged when a return understates the tax owed because of negligence or a substantial understatement.

Types of relief

How IRS penalty relief works

01

First-time abatement

Generally available if you had no significant penalties in the prior three years, have filed all required returns, and have paid or arranged to pay the tax.

02

Reasonable cause

Relief when something outside your control prevented you from filing or paying on time, such as serious illness, a natural disaster, or a death in the family.

03

Statutory exception

Relief when a penalty resulted from incorrect written advice you received from the IRS.

04

Request and follow up

Relief can be requested by phone, by letter, or on Form 843. We prepare the request and supporting documents for you.

Why the explanation matters

Reasonable-cause requests succeed or fail on the facts and documents behind them. A clear timeline and the right records, such as medical or insurance paperwork, make a real difference.

Penalty relief and other options

Penalty abatement can be combined with a payment plan or an Offer in Compromise. Lowering the balance first can make the rest of your plan easier to afford.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What is IRS first-time penalty abatement?+

It's an administrative waiver for taxpayers with a clean compliance history. If you qualify, the IRS can remove failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalties for one tax period.

Can the IRS remove interest?+

Interest on the tax itself generally can't be waived, but when a penalty is removed, the interest charged on that penalty is removed too.

Can I request penalty abatement if I still owe the tax?+

Yes. You can request relief while you are paying or have arranged to pay, although first-time abatement generally requires that payment be made or arranged.
